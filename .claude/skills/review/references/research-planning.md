# Optional Research and Paper Planning After Review

Use when a review request also asks whether or how to continue the research,
what evidence to collect next, or how the work could form a thesis or paper.
Ordinary assessment of significance and claim support remains part of review;
this addendum concerns future decisions and is optional.

## Preserve the completed assessment

Finish the source-bounded findings, anchor check and recommendation before
planning. Keep those findings and their declared source set identifiable. A
suggested future study cannot repair a current evidence gap until its results
exist and have actually been checked. Do not change a recommendation merely
because the proposed future work sounds promising.

In own-work mode, run the existing fresh-context review with only the submitted
manuscript and explicitly listed review evidence. Do not send desired future
claims, preferred conclusions, private planning notes or the drafting discussion
to the reviewer. After the reviewer returns, the coordinating agent may use the
user's goals and constraints for the addendum. Label that addendum as planning
advice from the coordinating agent, outside the clean-room assessment.

For another author's manuscript, keep advice within what the submitted material
allows. Do not invent that author's degree requirements, resources, intentions
or publication plans. An optional new research direction is not a defect in the
current paper merely because the author did not pursue it.

## Build the addendum from the actual findings

Use a heading such as **後續研究與論文規劃建議**, **后续研究与论文规划建议**
or **Research and paper planning advice**, matching the requested language.
Include only relevant items:

- What question is still worth investigating, and which inspected finding or
  source supports that judgement; identify missing grounding rather than
  asserting that a literature gap exists.
- What the present evidence can already support, which evidence a stronger
  contribution would require, and any known constraint affecting that work.
- A smallest useful next investigation, with a comparator or competing
  explanation, resource bound and consequences of supportive, non-supportive
  or ambiguous outcomes. Mark suggested bounds as suggestions.
- Separate thesis-level coherence from candidate single-paper contributions
  when those outputs were requested. Keep unknown degree or venue rules
  unknown; avoid chapter-to-paper assumptions and workload-based merit claims.
- The researcher's pending choice: continue, narrow, redirect, pause or stop,
  with the basis and what could change the advice. Do not infer author approval.

Point back to the relevant reviewed file and line or finding. Identify additional
planning inputs separately, for example a user-reported time or access limit;
do not retroactively present them as evidence read by the independent reviewer.
Keep required repairs to existing claims distinct from optional extensions.
Theoretical, explanatory, replication, synthesis, measurement, negative-result
and resource contributions remain valid possibilities; do not force a solution.

## Continue into a full plan only when requested

Keep a brief addendum in the review response. When the user asks for a full
research plan, a discussion record, or an update to an existing one, continue
with `/research-plan` using the findings and the explicitly supplied planning
inputs. That skill owns the discussion template, separate thesis and paper
fields, and preservation of prior versions. Reuse it instead of introducing a
second planning template or ledger. Existing authorisation for the requested
planning task is sufficient; do not ask for the same permission again.

If that skill is unavailable, still provide bounded advice in the response and
state that the full planning handoff was not executed. Do not claim to have
updated a saved plan. Neither the review nor this addendum authorises manuscript
rewriting, contacting collaborators or sending project materials elsewhere.
